# Motive and relationships

Literal relationship observations are useful from Phase 1 onward; use the phase matrix before forming cross-page identities or murder claims. Record who says or does what to whom, with pronoun ambiguity and narration/dialogue/quotation layer explicit.

Capture stated family/employment/intimacy ties, control of money/movement/information, and expressed feelings as V evidence. Link entity referents in the people view without merging them prematurely. Preserve alternative explanations: irritation is not murderous intent, dependence does not prove a conspiracy, and a quotation may not express the narrator's attitude.

In permitted later analysis, a motive hypothesis explains why a particular actor might act, but remains separate from opportunity, means, intentional killing and perpetrator identity. Cite the relevant claims and their revisions in any derived event conclusion. Use narrator profiling only when phase permits it; do not turn a relationship topic into a narrator label automatically.
