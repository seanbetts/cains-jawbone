# Narrator profiling

Follow the phase matrix: extraction can record page signatures; cross-page narrator claims begin with clustering. A page that does not fit an existing profile stays **unassigned**. Failure to match is not positive evidence of another narrator.

1. Describe page-specific narration: diction, syntax, register, knowledge, viewpoint, habits and attitudes. Separate quotation/dialogue speakers and detachable allusions from narrator voice; topic and scene can change without a narrator change.
2. Compare positive evidence for same and different voices, including plausible deliberate register changes. Record typed same-narrator or incompatible claims with exact spans. Identify what counterevidence would exclude each proposed assignment.
3. Assign an N ID only when the narrator hypothesis reaches supported or strongly_constrained status through affirmative evidence. Tentative positive profiles remain page-signature candidates linked by C IDs; keep the personal identity unnamed if unresolved. A shared hobby, quotation or meal is insufficient alone. Quotations can support an already evidenced voice pattern but are not automatically the narrator's own attitudes.
4. Maintain compact profile cards: V/C IDs, stable narration features, quote-layer features, exclusion tells, proposed pages, overlapping/unassigned pages, alternatives and the next discriminating test.
5. At every clustering review, compare competing partitions and consolidate justified duplicates without losing old IDs. Never prescribe a narrator count or force every page into a profile. A narrator-identity or narrator-assignment conclusion is a separate claim from same-narrator evidence.

No sequence is implied by profile membership. Test exclusions now; do not postpone until final falsification.
